01The metabolism myth

Most men over thirty walk into their first call convinced their metabolism is broken. The science is clear and far less dramatic. Total daily energy expenditure stays remarkably stable between twenty and sixty, with only a slight decline.

What actually changes is life. Career responsibility, hours at a desk, less unstructured movement, more business dinners, more travel, less sleep, and a default training program that has not evolved in a decade.

Your metabolism is not the problem. Your inputs and outputs are.

02What actually changes between 25 and 40

Three things shift quietly in the background. Daily activity drops, often by thousands of steps a day compared to your twenties. Recovery capacity changes, meaning you cannot abuse training and sleep the way you used to. Stress load multiplies, which directly impacts hunger, sleep and training quality.

Together, these create the illusion of a slowing metabolism. In reality, you are doing less, eating slightly more, recovering worse, and training without progression.

03The training mistake almost every man makes

Most men over thirty are still running a program designed for a twenty two year old with unlimited time. High volume, ego-led lifting, random splits, zero progression model.

Lean body composition past thirty demands smarter training. Lower volume, higher quality. Compound focused, progressively overloaded, with intelligent fatigue management. Two to four sessions a week, fully optimised, will outperform six chaotic ones.

04The nutrition pattern that quietly adds body fat

It is rarely the obvious meals that hold you back. It is the invisible calories. The flat white runs, the social drinks, the late dinners, the snacks during long meetings, the weekend resets.

Most professional men over thirty are sitting one to three hundred calories above maintenance for months at a time. That is enough to slowly add five to ten kilos a year without ever feeling like you overate.

05The simple reset

Set a precise calorie and protein target for your body and goal. Anchor four sessions of structured training per week with clear progression. Build a default day for travel, dinners and busy weeks. Sleep seven hours minimum.

Within six to twelve weeks, almost every man over thirty I have coached sees their body composition shift dramatically. It is not age. It is structure.
